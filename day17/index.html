Week 3 · Data Warehousing / Day 17

Day 17: Identify facts and dimensions from sample data

Wed 21 October 2026 · 28 queries · run on AdventureWorks2022, AdventureWorksDW2022 and AWPractice

Goal: given raw transactional tables never seen before, say what the fact is, what its grain should be, and which dimensions hang off it.

Method: Four questions in order: the business event (fact), the lowest level of detail (grain), the numbers (measures and their additivity), and what people filter or group by (dimensions).

QuestionWhat it gives youSales example
1. What business event is being measured?The factAn online sale
2. What is the lowest level of detail you would ever want?The grain: one row per what?One product on one order line
3. What numbers describe the event?The measures, each with its additivityOrderQty (additive), UnitPrice (non-additive)
4. What would someone filter or group by?The dimensions: who, what, where, when, howCustomer, product, territory, order date
Numbers that are not measures: a phone number, a postcode, an ID or a plate number is stored as digits but you would never add it up. Those are attributes (or keys), not measures. Test: would SUM of this column ever mean anything?
Where the queries ran: Exercises 1 and 2 read AdventureWorks2022 and AdventureWorksDW2022. Exercise 3 creates RawRides in AWPractice and then builds the star I designed (six dimensions and FactRide) so the self-test questions can be run, not just argued.

Exercise 1: sales, the one you know

#1

1. Q1 and Q2: the event and the grain in the OLTP tables

USE AdventureWorks2022;
SELECT COUNT(*) AS OrderLines, COUNT(DISTINCT h.SalesOrderID) AS Orders, SUM(d.LineTotal) AS Revenue
FROM Sales.SalesOrderDetail d
JOIN Sales.SalesOrderHeader h ON d.SalesOrderID = h.SalesOrderID
WHERE h.OnlineOrderFlag = 1;

What this query does

The business event is an online sale. The lowest level of detail is one product on one order, so the grain is the order line: 60,398 lines in 27,659 orders, 29,358,677.22 of revenue. Choosing the order (header) as the grain would lose which products were bought, so the line wins.

📍 Pick the lowest level anyone could ask about. You can always add lines up to orders, but you can never split an order back into its lines.

#2

2. Q3: the measures on a line

SELECT MIN(d.OrderQty) AS MinQty, MAX(d.OrderQty) AS MaxQty,
       SUM(CASE WHEN d.UnitPriceDiscount > 0 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS DiscountedLines,
       MIN(d.UnitPrice) AS MinPrice, MAX(d.UnitPrice) AS MaxPrice
FROM Sales.SalesOrderDetail d
JOIN Sales.SalesOrderHeader h ON d.SalesOrderID = h.SalesOrderID
WHERE h.OnlineOrderFlag = 1;

What this query does

The line carries OrderQty, UnitPrice, UnitPriceDiscount and LineTotal. Online, every line has quantity 1 and no line is discounted, so LineTotal equals UnitPrice here. OrderQty and LineTotal are additive; UnitPrice and the discount percentage are not (they are rates, recompute them from totals). Product cost comes from Production.Product.StandardCost, so a warehouse would copy it onto the line at load time.

📍 Quantity and amount are measures. Price and discount percentage describe one line and must be recomputed, not summed.

#3

3. Q3: header numbers that belong to the whole order

SELECT SalesOrderID, OrderDate, DueDate, ShipDate, CustomerID, TerritoryID,
       SubTotal, TaxAmt, Freight, TotalDue
FROM Sales.SalesOrderHeader
WHERE SalesOrderID = 43697;

What this query does

TaxAmt and Freight are stored once per order, not per line. At a line grain they either have to be split across the lines (allocated) or kept in a separate order-level fact. The header also gives three dates (order, due, ship), the customer and the territory: those are dimensions (Q4: when, who, where).

📍 A measure recorded at a higher level than the grain is a design decision: allocate it to the lines or keep it in its own fact.

#4

4. Compare: the dimensions Microsoft attached to FactInternetSales

USE AdventureWorksDW2022;
SELECT COL_NAME(fkc.parent_object_id, fkc.parent_column_id) AS FactColumn,
       OBJECT_NAME(fk.referenced_object_id) AS Dimension
FROM sys.foreign_keys fk
JOIN sys.foreign_key_columns fkc ON fkc.constraint_object_id = fk.object_id
WHERE fk.parent_object_id = OBJECT_ID('dbo.FactInternetSales')
ORDER BY Dimension;

What this query does

The foreign keys are the dimensions. My answer from the source tables was Product, Customer, Date (three roles), Territory. Microsoft's has those plus DimCurrency and DimPromotion. Currency comes from Sales.CurrencyRate and promotion from SpecialOffer, which were not in this exercise's table list, so the difference comes from the inputs rather than a different method. DimDate appears three times (order, due, ship): one dimension in three roles.

📍 If you were only given six tables, five dimensions is the right answer. Microsoft had more source tables, so it had more dimensions to attach.

#5

5. Compare: same grain, and the header measures were allocated to the lines

SELECT COUNT(*) AS FactRows, COUNT(DISTINCT SalesOrderNumber) AS Orders,
       SUM(SalesAmount) AS Revenue, SUM(TaxAmt) AS Tax, SUM(Freight) AS Freight,
       (SELECT SUM(TaxAmt) FROM AdventureWorks2022.Sales.SalesOrderHeader WHERE OnlineOrderFlag = 1) AS OltpTax,
       (SELECT SUM(Freight) FROM AdventureWorks2022.Sales.SalesOrderHeader WHERE OnlineOrderFlag = 1) AS OltpFreight
FROM FactInternetSales;

What this query does

60,398 rows and 27,659 orders: Microsoft chose the same grain, one row per order line. Revenue matches to the cent. TaxAmt and Freight are on every line, so Microsoft allocated the header amounts down to the lines: tax adds back up exactly (2,348,694.2301), freight is 1.94 higher than the OLTP total (733,969.61 vs 733,967.67), which is rounding when an order's freight is split across its lines.

📍 Microsoft was right to keep the line grain and allocate tax and freight, and the 1.94 gap shows the price: allocated measures can drift by rounding. Always reconcile them.

Exercise 2: HR

#6

6. SELECT TOP 20 * FROM HumanResources.Employee

USE AdventureWorks2022;
SELECT TOP 20 * FROM HumanResources.Employee;

What this query does

One row per employee (290): national ID, login, job title, birth and hire dates, marital status, gender, salaried flag, vacation and sick hours. These are descriptions of a person, not events, so this table becomes the employee dimension. VacationHours and SickLeaveHours are balances, a snapshot of today.

📍 A table with one row per thing and many descriptive columns is a dimension source.

What this query does

One row per employee per department assignment: department, shift, start date and end date (NULL means current). 296 rows for 290 people, so 5 people changed department (Sheela Word twice). Each row records an event that happened at a date: someone joined a department on a shift.

📍 StartDate and EndDate on a row are the sign of history, and history is where facts hide.

📍 Small lookup tables usually become dimension attributes.

#9

9. SELECT TOP 20 * FROM HumanResources.Shift

USE AdventureWorks2022;
SELECT TOP 20 * FROM HumanResources.Shift;

What this query does

Three rows: Day 07:00 to 15:00, Evening 15:00 to 23:00, Night 23:00 to 07:00. It describes when someone works, so it becomes a tiny dimension (DimShift), or an attribute of the assignment.

📍 Three rows is too small to need its own table in a star, but it is a dimension either way.

#10

10. SELECT TOP 20 * FROM HumanResources.EmployeePayHistory

USE AdventureWorks2022;
SELECT TOP 20 * FROM HumanResources.EmployeePayHistory;

What this query does

One row per employee per pay change: RateChangeDate, Rate (hourly) and PayFrequency (1 = monthly, 2 = biweekly). The primary key is BusinessEntityID + RateChangeDate.

📍 This is the pay-change event. Its grain is already visible in the key: one employee on one change date.

#11

11. The grain of a pay-change fact

SELECT Records AS PayChanges, COUNT(*) AS Employees
FROM (SELECT BusinessEntityID, COUNT(*) AS Records
      FROM HumanResources.EmployeePayHistory GROUP BY BusinessEntityID) x
GROUP BY Records;

What this query does

316 pay records: 277 employees have one rate and 13 have three. The grain of a pay-change fact is one row per employee per rate change (BusinessEntityID + RateChangeDate is unique). Dimensions: Employee, Date of the change, Department at the time, Pay frequency.

📍 State the grain as a sentence: "one row per employee per rate change".

#12

12. Is Rate additive? No

SELECT BusinessEntityID, COUNT(*) AS PayRecords, SUM(Rate) AS SumOfRates, MAX(Rate) AS LatestRate
FROM HumanResources.EmployeePayHistory
WHERE BusinessEntityID = 4
GROUP BY BusinessEntityID;

What this query does

Rob Walters (employee 4) earned 8.62, then 23.72, then 29.85 an hour. Summing gives 62.19 an hour, a rate he never had. Rate is non-additive: you can average it, take the latest, or compute the change (new rate minus old rate, which is additive across employees). A pay-change fact would store Rate plus RateChange as the measures.

📍 An hourly rate is a price. Like UnitPrice on Day 16, never SUM it.

#13

13. The second fact: department transfers

SELECT h.BusinessEntityID, p.FirstName, p.LastName, d.Name AS Department, s.Name AS Shift, h.StartDate, h.EndDate
FROM HumanResources.EmployeeDepartmentHistory h
JOIN Person.Person p ON p.BusinessEntityID = h.BusinessEntityID
JOIN HumanResources.Department d ON d.DepartmentID = h.DepartmentID
JOIN HumanResources.Shift s ON s.ShiftID = h.ShiftID
WHERE h.BusinessEntityID IN (SELECT BusinessEntityID FROM HumanResources.EmployeeDepartmentHistory GROUP BY BusinessEntityID HAVING COUNT(*) > 1)
ORDER BY h.BusinessEntityID, h.StartDate;

What this query does

The five people who moved: Rob Walters (Engineering to Tool Design), David Bradley, William Vong, Laura Norman and Sheela Word (three departments). This is the second fact: an assignment (or transfer) event with grain one row per employee per department assignment. It has no numbers to add, so it is a factless fact; you COUNT rows to get transfers or headcount on a date.

📍 Two events, two facts: pay changes (with a measure) and assignments (factless).

#14

14. Shift becomes a dimension

SELECT s.Name AS Shift, s.StartTime, s.EndTime, COUNT(*) AS CurrentEmployees
FROM HumanResources.EmployeeDepartmentHistory h
JOIN HumanResources.Shift s ON s.ShiftID = h.ShiftID
WHERE h.EndDate IS NULL
GROUP BY s.Name, s.StartTime, s.EndTime
ORDER BY s.StartTime;

What this query does

176 people on the day shift, 62 evening and 52 night (290 current assignments). Shift is something you group by, so it is a dimension: DimShift with three rows (name, start time, end time), attached to the assignment fact. It is small enough that a pure star would fold it into the assignment as a column.

📍 Anything you would put on a report's rows or a slicer is a dimension, however small.

#15

15. DimEmployee: history rows for the same five people

USE AdventureWorksDW2022;
SELECT EmployeeKey, FirstName, LastName, DepartmentName, Title, BaseRate, StartDate, EndDate, Status
FROM DimEmployee
WHERE EmployeeNationalIDAlternateKey IN (SELECT EmployeeNationalIDAlternateKey FROM DimEmployee GROUP BY EmployeeNationalIDAlternateKey HAVING COUNT(*) > 1)
ORDER BY LastName, StartDate;

What this query does

DimEmployee has 296 rows for 290 people: the extra 6 rows belong to exactly the five people who moved department in the OLTP (Sheela Word has three rows). So Microsoft kept the transfers as history rows in the dimension (slowly changing dimension, Day 20+). But every version carries the same, current department and rate, and the dates do not match the OLTP transfer dates, so the history in the sample is only structural.

📍 Microsoft put the HR events into the dimension as versions, not into a fact.

#16

16. DimEmployee keeps only the latest pay rate

SELECT COUNT(*) AS CurrentRows,
       SUM(CASE WHEN d.BaseRate = lr.Rate THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS BaseRateIsLatestRate
FROM DimEmployee d
JOIN AdventureWorks2022.HumanResources.Employee e ON e.NationalIDNumber = d.EmployeeNationalIDAlternateKey
OUTER APPLY (SELECT TOP 1 ph.Rate FROM AdventureWorks2022.HumanResources.EmployeePayHistory ph
             WHERE ph.BusinessEntityID = e.BusinessEntityID ORDER BY ph.RateChangeDate DESC) lr
WHERE d.Status = 'Current';

What this query does

All 290 current rows have BaseRate equal to the employee's most recent rate in EmployeePayHistory. The 26 earlier rates of the 13 people with raises are not in the warehouse anywhere. The pay-change fact I designed above simply does not exist in AdventureWorksDW.

📍 If a question needs history and the warehouse only kept the latest value, the warehouse cannot answer it.

#17

17. No HR fact in the warehouse

SELECT name AS FactTable FROM sys.tables WHERE name LIKE 'Fact%' ORDER BY name;

What this query does

Ten fact tables: sales (internet, reseller, reasons), quotas, finance, currency rates, inventory, call centre, survey responses and product descriptions. None measures pay or transfers. FactSalesQuota and FactResellerSales use DimEmployee only as the salesperson. DimEmployee exists to describe who sold, not to analyse HR.

📍 The warehouse was built for sales analysis. Employees are there as a dimension of sales.

19. Look at the raw data

SELECT * FROM RawRides;

What this query does

Five rides over two days. The four questions: (1) the event is a completed ride; (2) the grain is one row per ride (RideID); (3) the numbers are DistanceKm, FareAmount, TipAmount and Rating; (4) people would group by rider, driver, vehicle, pickup and dropoff zone, date, time of day, payment method and promo.

#20

20. What the raw table repeats

SELECT 'Rider' AS Entity, RiderPhone AS Id, RiderName + ', ' + RiderCity AS Description, COUNT(*) AS TimesStored
FROM RawRides GROUP BY RiderPhone, RiderName, RiderCity
UNION ALL
SELECT 'Driver + vehicle', CAST(DriverID AS VARCHAR(10)), DriverName + ', ' + VehicleType + ' ' + VehiclePlate, COUNT(*)
FROM RawRides GROUP BY DriverID, DriverName, VehicleType, VehiclePlate
ORDER BY Entity, TimesStored DESC;

What this query does

Ayesha's name and city are stored twice, Bilal's twice, and Nadeem with his Bike LEC-5678 twice. With five rows that is harmless; with five million, a rider changing city means updating every one of their rides, and a typo in one row splits a rider in two. A dimension stores each rider once and the fact points at it by key.

📍 Repeated descriptive text in a raw table is a dimension waiting to be pulled out.

#21

21. Build the dimensions

DROP TABLE IF EXISTS dbo.FactRide, dbo.DimRider, dbo.DimDriver, dbo.DimVehicle, dbo.DimZone, dbo.DimRideDate, dbo.DimPayment;
CREATE TABLE DimRider (RiderKey INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY, RiderPhone VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL UNIQUE, RiderName VARCHAR(50), RiderCity VARCHAR(50));
CREATE TABLE DimDriver (DriverKey INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY, DriverID INT NOT NULL UNIQUE, DriverName VARCHAR(50));
CREATE TABLE DimVehicle (VehicleKey INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY, VehiclePlate VARCHAR(10) NOT NULL UNIQUE, VehicleType VARCHAR(20));
CREATE TABLE DimZone (ZoneKey INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY, ZoneName VARCHAR(30) NOT NULL, City VARCHAR(50), CONSTRAINT UQ_Zone UNIQUE (ZoneName, City));
CREATE TABLE DimRideDate (DateKey INT PRIMARY KEY, FullDate DATE, DayName VARCHAR(10), MonthName VARCHAR(10), CalendarYear INT);
CREATE TABLE DimPayment (PaymentKey INT IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY, PaymentMethod VARCHAR(10), PromoCode VARCHAR(10), HasPromo BIT);
INSERT INTO DimRider (RiderPhone, RiderName, RiderCity) SELECT DISTINCT RiderPhone, RiderName, RiderCity FROM RawRides;
INSERT INTO DimDriver (DriverID, DriverName) SELECT DISTINCT DriverID, DriverName FROM RawRides;
INSERT INTO DimVehicle (VehiclePlate, VehicleType) SELECT DISTINCT VehiclePlate, VehicleType FROM RawRides;
INSERT INTO DimZone (ZoneName, City) SELECT PickupZone, RiderCity FROM RawRides UNION SELECT DropoffZone, RiderCity FROM RawRides;
INSERT INTO DimRideDate SELECT DISTINCT CONVERT(INT, FORMAT(PickupTime, 'yyyyMMdd')), CAST(PickupTime AS DATE),
       DATENAME(weekday, PickupTime), DATENAME(month, PickupTime), YEAR(PickupTime) FROM RawRides;
INSERT INTO DimPayment (PaymentMethod, PromoCode, HasPromo)
SELECT DISTINCT PaymentMethod, PromoCode, CASE WHEN PromoCode IS NULL THEN 0 ELSE 1 END FROM RawRides;

What this query does

Six dimensions, each with an integer surrogate key and the natural key kept as a unique column: 3 riders (by phone), 4 drivers, 4 vehicles (by plate), 6 zones, 2 dates and 4 payment/promo combinations. Vehicle is separate from driver because a car can change hands. Zone carries the city (the raw table only has the rider's city, so a zone takes the city of the rides it appears in). Payment method and promo code are both tiny, so they share one "junk" dimension instead of two one-column tables.

📍 Each DISTINCT pulls one entity out of the raw table. The UNIQUE constraint on the natural key stops a second copy creeping in on the next load.

#22

22. Build and load the fact

DROP TABLE IF EXISTS dbo.FactRide;
CREATE TABLE FactRide (
    RideID INT PRIMARY KEY,
    DateKey INT REFERENCES DimRideDate(DateKey), PickupHour TINYINT,
    RiderKey INT REFERENCES DimRider(RiderKey), DriverKey INT REFERENCES DimDriver(DriverKey),
    VehicleKey INT REFERENCES DimVehicle(VehicleKey),
    PickupZoneKey INT REFERENCES DimZone(ZoneKey), DropoffZoneKey INT REFERENCES DimZone(ZoneKey),
    PaymentKey INT REFERENCES DimPayment(PaymentKey),
    PickupTime DATETIME, DropoffTime DATETIME,
    DistanceKm DECIMAL(6,2), DurationMin INT, FareAmount DECIMAL(8,2), TipAmount DECIMAL(8,2), Rating TINYINT
);
INSERT INTO FactRide
SELECT r.RideID, CONVERT(INT, FORMAT(r.PickupTime, 'yyyyMMdd')), DATEPART(hour, r.PickupTime),
       dr.RiderKey, dd.DriverKey, v.VehicleKey, pz.ZoneKey, dz.ZoneKey, pm.PaymentKey,
       r.PickupTime, r.DropoffTime, r.DistanceKm, DATEDIFF(minute, r.PickupTime, r.DropoffTime),
       r.FareAmount, r.TipAmount, r.Rating
FROM RawRides r
JOIN DimRider dr ON dr.RiderPhone = r.RiderPhone
JOIN DimDriver dd ON dd.DriverID = r.DriverID
JOIN DimVehicle v ON v.VehiclePlate = r.VehiclePlate
JOIN DimZone pz ON pz.ZoneName = r.PickupZone AND pz.City = r.RiderCity
JOIN DimZone dz ON dz.ZoneName = r.DropoffZone AND dz.City = r.RiderCity
JOIN DimPayment pm ON pm.PaymentMethod = r.PaymentMethod AND ISNULL(pm.PromoCode, '') = ISNULL(r.PromoCode, '');

What this query does

One row per ride. RideID stays in the fact as a degenerate dimension (it identifies the ride but has no attributes of its own). DimZone is joined twice, once as pickup and once as dropoff: a role-playing dimension like DimDate in FactInternetSales. The load swaps every natural key for the dimension's surrogate key and computes DurationMin once. The promo join uses ISNULL because NULL = NULL is not true. "5 rows affected".

📍 If the fact row count after the load differs from the raw row count, a dimension lookup failed. Five in, five out.

#24

24. Rides per zone per day

SELECT d.FullDate, z.ZoneName AS PickupZone, COUNT(*) AS Rides
FROM FactRide f
JOIN DimRideDate d ON d.DateKey = f.DateKey
JOIN DimZone z ON z.ZoneKey = f.PickupZoneKey
GROUP BY d.FullDate, z.ZoneName
ORDER BY d.FullDate, z.ZoneName;

What this query does

Five rows, one ride each. Counting rows of the fact is the measure here: the grain is one ride, so COUNT(*) is the number of rides. Swap PickupZoneKey for DropoffZoneKey to count by destination.

📍 No join to RawRides needed.

#25

25. Average tip by payment method

SELECT p.PaymentMethod, COUNT(*) AS Rides, AVG(f.TipAmount) AS AvgTip
FROM FactRide f
JOIN DimPayment p ON p.PaymentKey = f.PaymentKey
GROUP BY p.PaymentMethod
ORDER BY AvgTip DESC;

What this query does

Wallet 100 (one ride), Card 25, Cash 10. TipAmount is additive, so AVG works on it directly.

📍 Group by a dimension attribute, aggregate a measure: the pattern for every report.

#26

26. Top drivers by fare

SELECT d.DriverName, COUNT(*) AS Rides, SUM(f.FareAmount) AS Fare
FROM FactRide f
JOIN DimDriver d ON d.DriverKey = f.DriverKey
GROUP BY d.DriverName
ORDER BY Fare DESC;

What this query does

Kamran 700, Faisal 520, Tariq 450, Nadeem 360 (from two rides). FareAmount is additive across rides, drivers and dates.

📍 Group by the key's attribute (name) only when names are unique; with real data group by DriverKey and show the name.

#27

27. Repeat riders

SELECT r.RiderName, r.RiderPhone, COUNT(*) AS Rides
FROM FactRide f
JOIN DimRider r ON r.RiderKey = f.RiderKey
GROUP BY r.RiderName, r.RiderPhone
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;

What this query does

Ayesha and Bilal each rode twice. This works because DimRider identifies a rider by phone number, so the same person always gets the same RiderKey.

📍 All four self-test questions run on the star alone: the model is complete for them.

#28

28. Additivity check: Rating and DistanceKm

SELECT SUM(Rating) AS SumOfRatings, AVG(CAST(Rating AS DECIMAL(4,2))) AS AvgRating,
       SUM(DistanceKm) AS TotalKm, SUM(FareAmount) / SUM(DistanceKm) AS FarePerKm
FROM FactRide;

What this query does

SUM(Rating) gives 22, which means nothing; the average, 4.4, does. Rating is non-additive. DistanceKm is additive (41.8 km in total), and a rate such as fare per km (48.56) is computed from the two sums, never stored and summed. CAST makes AVG return decimals: AVG of a tinyint would round down to 4.

📍 Rating: average it. DistanceKm, FareAmount, TipAmount, DurationMin: sum them.

Exercise 2 notes: HR

QuestionAnswer
What is the fact? (there are two)(1) A pay change (EmployeePayHistory): an employee's rate changed on a date. (2) A department assignment (EmployeeDepartmentHistory): an employee joined a department on a shift on a date. The second has no numbers, so it is a factless fact: you count rows to get transfers or headcount.
Grain of a pay-change factOne row per employee per rate change (BusinessEntityID + RateChangeDate is the key): 316 rows, 277 people with one rate, 13 with three.
Is Rate additive?No. It is an hourly price. Rob Walters's three rates sum to 62.19 an hour, a rate he never had. Average it, take the latest, or store the change (new minus old), which is additive.
What does Shift become?A dimension, DimShift: 3 rows (Day, Evening, Night with start and end times), attached to the assignment fact. It is small enough that a pure star would fold it into the fact or a junk dimension.
DimEmployee in the warehouse296 rows for 290 people. The 6 extra rows belong to exactly the 5 people who changed department in the OLTP, so transfers became history versions of the dimension. BaseRate equals the latest pay rate for all 290 current rows; the 26 earlier rates are not stored anywhere in the warehouse.
Why did Microsoft not build an HR fact? AdventureWorksDW is a sales warehouse: every fact table measures selling, finance or customers, and DimEmployee is there to answer "who sold it" for FactResellerSales and FactSalesQuota. The HR data is tiny (290 employees, 316 pay rows, 296 assignments), so HR questions can be answered straight from the OLTP database without a warehouse. Pay is also sensitive, and keeping it out of a widely shared reporting database is a reasonable choice. A real HR warehouse would add a headcount snapshot fact (one row per employee per month) and a pay-change fact.

Exercise 3 notes: ride-sharing

Fact and grain: the event is a completed ride. Grain: one row per ride (RideID). Five raw rows become five fact rows.
MeasureAdditive?Why
FareAmountAdditiveFares add up across rides, drivers, zones and days (2,030 in total).
TipAmountAdditiveSame: sum it, or average it per payment method.
DistanceKmAdditiveKilometres add up (41.8 km). Ratios such as fare per km are computed from the sums.
DurationMinAdditiveDerived: DropoffTime minus PickupTime, computed at load (see the stretch question).
RatingNon-additiveA score from 1 to 5. The sum (22) means nothing; the average (4.4) does.
Ride countAdditiveNot a column: COUNT(*) on the fact, because the grain is one ride.
DimensionAttributesOwn table or folded in?
RiderRiderPhone (natural key), RiderName, RiderCityOwn table (DimRider): riders repeat and change city.
DriverDriverID, DriverNameOwn table (DimDriver).
VehicleVehiclePlate, VehicleTypeOwn table (DimVehicle): a car can change driver. Folded into the driver in a pure star (Day 19).
ZoneZoneName, CityOwn table (DimZone), used twice: pickup and dropoff (role-playing).
DateFullDate, DayName, MonthName, CalendarYearOwn table (DimRideDate), key yyyymmdd like DimDate.
Time of dayPickupHourFolded into the fact as PickupHour; a 24-row DimTimeOfDay (hour, morning peak, evening peak) is the next step.
Payment methodCard, Cash, WalletToo small for its own table: combined with promo into one junk dimension.
PromoPromoCode, HasPromoSame junk dimension (DimPayment, 4 combinations).
Degenerate dimension: RideID stays in the fact: it identifies the ride but has no attributes of its own. What the raw table duplicates: Ayesha's name and city twice, Bilal's twice, Nadeem with his bike LEC-5678 twice. In the star each is stored once and the fact points at it by key, so a change of city is one UPDATE, not one per ride.

Stretch: store ride duration, or compute it at query time?

For storing it: duration is computed once at load, every report and Power BI visual gets the same number, and it can be summed and averaged like any other measure without repeating DATEDIFF everywhere.
For computing it: it is fully derived from PickupTime and DropoffTime, so storing it duplicates data that can drift if a timestamp is corrected later, and the formula (minutes or seconds? rounding?) can change without reloading.
Decision: store DurationMin in the fact, computed in the load from the two timestamps, and keep the timestamps too. Reports should not each reinvent a measure, and if a timestamp is corrected the same load recomputes it.

Self-test: does the sketch answer the four questions?

Yes, all four ran against the star with no join to RawRides (queries 24 to 27): rides per zone per day (FactRide + DimRideDate + DimZone), average tip by payment method (FactRide + DimPayment), top drivers by fare (FactRide + DimDriver) and repeat riders (FactRide + DimRider, grouped by phone).
